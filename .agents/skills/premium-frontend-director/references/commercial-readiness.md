# Commercial Readiness

Use this guide when the user wants a premium, client-ready website or refers to a high-value target such as “$10k+ quality.” Treat that phrase as a standard for scope, strategy, craft, and delivery rigor—not as a claim that the finished work has a particular price or will produce a specific business result.

## Define the value proposition before building

Write down the client and audience, business problem, desired user actions, measurable success signals, scope, constraints, and acceptance evidence. Separate included work from assumptions, optional work, and later phases. If the budget or scope is unknown, describe the work needed to reach the target and avoid quoting or promising a price.

## Premium delivery checks

Scale these checks to the agreed scope; do not add irrelevant features just to make a project appear expensive.

- **Strategy:** The information architecture and primary user journeys reflect verified client goals, audience needs, and competitive context.
- **Distinctiveness:** A coherent art direction and reusable design system fit the actual brand and content; the result is not a generic theme or a copy of a reference site.
- **Content and trust:** Business claims, portfolio work, testimonials, credentials, addresses, and imagery are authentic or clearly marked as client-supplied placeholders awaiting approval.
- **Conversion and usability:** Calls to action, forms, navigation, empty/error/loading states, and contact paths work end to end and have clear user feedback.
- **Production quality:** Responsive behavior, keyboard access, focus visibility, reduced motion, semantic structure, SEO fundamentals, performance, privacy/security needs, and browser errors are checked with tools appropriate to the project.
- **Operations:** CMS roles and content workflows, integrations, analytics/measurement, deployment assumptions, maintenance ownership, and rollback/recovery needs are documented when in scope.
- **Handoff:** Provide the agreed source/design artifacts, component or content guidance, test evidence, known limitations, and client handoff steps.

## Evidence and claims

- Attach each acceptance criterion to an observable artifact or test. Mark any untested item as not verified.
- Do not use a design score, GitHub comparison, or polished screenshot as proof of market price, conversion lift, revenue, or ROI.
- Claim performance or accessibility results only when measured on the actual deliverable using stated conditions. Keep lab results separate from field outcomes.
- A $10k+ fee depends on the buyer, scope, strategy, content, integrations, risk, support, and demonstrated business value. The Skill can help define and deliver a credible premium scope; it cannot guarantee the buyer's willingness to pay.
